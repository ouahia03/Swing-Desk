Connect your free market data

Swing Desk uses three free data services. Each takes about two minutes to set up, and none needs a credit card.

Twelve Data (price history) Connected

  1. Go to twelvedata.com and click Sign up.
  2. Confirm your email if asked, then log in.
  3. Open the API Keys page from the menu and copy your key.

Finnhub (live prices, news, earnings) Connected

  1. Go to finnhub.io and click Get free API key.
  2. Sign up with your email and confirm it if asked.
  3. Your key appears on the dashboard right after you log in. Copy it.

Massive (daily prices for all 500 stocks) Connected

  1. Go to massive.com (formerly Polygon.io) and click Sign up or Get your free API key.
  2. Create an account with your email and choose the free Basic plan if asked.
  3. Open API Keys in your dashboard and copy the default key.

Keys are saved only in this browser on this device. They are never written into the file. The optional AI key can be added later in Settings.

      How Swing Desk works

      Every stock gets a score from 1 to 100 built from ten factors, weighted by how much they matter for a 1 to 4 week swing trade. The score leads to one of three verdicts.

      Good buyScore 70 or higher, and no deal-breakers.
      I would waitScore 45 to 69, or a strong stock with a deal-breaker.
      Not a good buyScore below 45, or the stock is in a clear downtrend.

      Deal-breakers keep a high score from becoming a Good buy: reward to risk worse than 2:1 at today's price, earnings within the next two weeks, RSI above 75, or a market selloff. When one applies, the verdict tells you what to wait for.

      Top buys

      The dashboard scores every stock in your scan list (500 by default) plus your watchlist, and shows up to five that currently rate Good buy, highest score first. If fewer qualify, it shows fewer. Some days the honest answer is none.

      The AI scan

      Click Run AI scan on the dashboard whenever you want. It gathers recent news for every stock, then has Claude Haiku review them 10 at a time using your framework. Each stock gets an AI verdict, a one-line reason and a short summary, which appear on its page and in the Scan Results tab. A full scan of 750 stocks takes about 15 minutes and costs roughly $1.35. You can stop it at any time, and results so far are kept. A brand-new Claude account may start with lower speed limits, so the first few scans can take a little longer.

      Analyze with AI

      This button, near the top of each stock's page, gives a deeper look at one stock: the full news, earnings history and every factor. It's about half a cent with Haiku.

      Scan Results tab

      Lists every scanned stock with its score, verdict, AI verdict and key numbers. Sort and filter to narrow it down, for example by sector or by stocks where the AI disagrees with the score. Download spreadsheet saves what you see as a file that opens in Excel or Google Sheets.

      Setting up the AI

      Go to console.anthropic.com and create an account. Under Billing, add a small amount of prepaid credit, and set a monthly spend limit so it can never charge more than you choose. Then open API Keys, click Create Key, copy it, and paste it into Settings. The daily AI spending limit in Settings adds a second safety net.

      My Trades

      Log each purchase with Log a trade (or Log a purchase on a stock's page), and add the sale when you sell. When you log a purchase, the site saves a snapshot of the conditions at that moment: score, verdict, distance from support, earnings timing, market and sector trend, news and the AI's view. Past trades get a snapshot rebuilt from price history and news from that time (up to about 6 to 7 months back).

      "What your trades show" compares your win rate under different conditions. Suggested rule changes appear after 20 closed trades, and you decide whether to apply them. With fewer trades, patterns can easily be luck.

      Trade alerts

      If an open trade falls to your stop, reaches your target, or its verdict drops to Not a good buy, a banner appears at the top of the site. Turn on pop-up notifications in Settings to also get a browser pop-up while the site is open.

      Setting up sync between computers

      1. Sign in at github.com, click your profile picture (top right), then Settings.
      2. Scroll down the left menu to Developer settings, then Personal access tokens, then Tokens (classic).
      3. Click Generate new token, then Generate new token (classic).
      4. In Note, type Swing Desk sync. Pick an Expiration (90 days is a good choice) and write down the date.
      5. Under scopes, check only the box named gist. Scroll down and click Generate token.
      6. Copy the token right away (GitHub shows it only once). In Swing Desk, open Settings, paste it into GitHub token, enter its expiration date under Key expiration reminders, and click Save settings.
      7. On each other computer, paste the same token into Settings. Your trades and watchlist load within a few seconds.

      Only your trades and watchlist sync. Your API keys never leave each browser.

      The final verdict

      The big verdict on each stock combines up to three opinions: the chart score, your AI scan, and a detailed analysis if you ran one. If only the chart score is available, it decides. With two, agreement wins; one step apart, the more cautious view wins; opposite views give I would wait. With three, two of three decide, and if all three differ the result is I would wait. Deal-breakers (earnings in your window, an AI red flag, poor reward to risk, a market selloff, or a longer-term downtrend with price just under its 50-day average) can't be outvoted into a Good buy. AI opinions count for 3 trading days. A stock is only called Not a good buy by the chart when the score is below 45 or the price is more than 3% below a 50-day average that is itself below the 200-day.

      Financials

      Each stock's page shows revenue, expenses, net income and net margin from the company's SEC filings (last 5 quarters or 5 fiscal years), expected vs. actual earnings per share for the last 4 quarters, and a fundamentals table colored green when a number improved and red when it got worse. Guidance comes from the AI's read of recent headlines, so it's only as complete as the news.

      News in the score

      Before an AI scan, the Catalyst factor counts how much news a stock has. After a scan, the AI's rating of the news (from strong positive to negative) replaces that count, and the score updates. If the AI spots serious negative news, like a guidance cut or an investigation, the stock can't be a Good buy. News ratings count for 3 trading days.

      Scoring rules

      The numbers behind the deal-breakers (the score needed for a Good buy, the earnings window, minimum reward to risk and the RSI limit) can be changed in Settings. Suggested changes from your trades use the same settings.

      Terms you'll see

      20, 50 and 200-day averages
      The average closing price over that many trading days. Price above rising averages usually means an uptrend.
      Higher highs and higher lows
      Each bounce peaks higher and each dip bottoms higher. That's the basic shape of an uptrend.
      Support and resistance
      Support is a price where buyers have stepped in before; resistance is where sellers have. Dashed lines on the chart mark the nearest of each.
      Reward to risk
      How far the stock could rise to its target, compared with how far it could fall to its stop. 2:1 means you could make twice what you risk.
      Relative strength
      How the stock has done compared with the S&P 500 (SPY) and its sector over the past month.
      RSI
      A 0 to 100 gauge of how stretched recent moves are. It's judged against the trend: 30 in a downtrend can mean more selling, not a bounce.
      MACD
      A momentum gauge. Above its signal line and rising means momentum is improving.
      ATR (daily range)
      How much the stock typically moves in a day. Used to place stops and targets sensibly.
      Accumulation
      Volume on up days compared with down days. More volume on up days suggests buyers are building positions.

      How the data loads

      The site scans the 750 most heavily traded US stocks priced above $5 (by average daily dollar volume), plus your watchlist, open trades and any extras you add. The list refreshes monthly. Massive provides one day of prices for every stock in a single request. The very first time on each computer, the site downloads about 1.5 years of history one day at a time (5 days a minute on the free plan), which takes about 80 minutes, once. Stocks can be scored after the first 60 days, about 15 minutes in. After that, each new trading day takes one request, so opening the page each morning takes a couple of minutes. Massive's free plan is end-of-day data, so the morning ranking uses the previous close; during market hours, Finnhub keeps your watchlist and the 50 strongest stocks up to date.

      Finnhub provides news, earnings dates, company names and live prices, and allows about 60 requests a minute. Twelve Data provides the sector funds and fills in any stock added later. Everything only loads while this page is open.

      Making changes later

      Ask Claude for the change and download the new file. Save it with the same name, in the same folder, replacing the old one, and open it in the same browser. Your keys and lists are stored in the browser, so they carry over. If anything goes missing, use Restore from backup in Settings.
